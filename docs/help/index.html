Connection

Getting connected

Projector Pilot talks to your projector directly on your network. Three things get you connected, and one of them trips most people up.

Same Wi-Fi, same subnet

The phone and the projector must be on the same network and subnet. Guest networks, IoT VLANs and some mesh setups isolate devices from each other, so a projector on the "IoT" SSID cannot be reached from your phone on the main one. Put both on the same network.

Enable PJLink and Web Control on the projector

Network control has to be turned on at the projector. In the projector's network menu, enable PJLink and Web Control (Epson calls the latter the web remote / Web Control). With network control off, the projector simply does not answer.

Find the projector's IP address

The app scans your network for projectors, and you can also add one by IP. To find the IP, open the projector's network information screen in its own menu - it lists the current address. If your scan comes up empty, add that IP by hand.

The three projector passwords

Epson projectors have three separate passwords: Web Control, PJLink and Remote. They are not the same field, and a projector can want one, some or none of them. Enter the ones your projector asks for; the app works out which the projector actually requires and connects either way, and Diagnostics tells you which path was used. If a projector answers without needing a password, the app says the saved password was not needed here rather than claiming none is set.

Compatibility

Supported projectors

  • Epson projectors over ESC/VP21 and Web Control - the two together give you power, the full picture menu, geometry and motorized lens control.
  • Any PJLink projector - power, input and status over the PJLink standard, so a non-Epson or a PJLink-only Epson is an ordinary supported device.
  • A transport is used because it answers, never because it was configured. The app prefers ESC/VP21, then PJLink, then Web Control, but it falls back to whatever the projector actually responds on. A projector with no Web Control keeps working over PJLink; a value a transport refuses is treated as unknown, not as unsupported.
Not sure about your model? The app's About screen lists the projectors it supports from its model database, and says for each whether it was verified on hardware or inferred. If the projector's own web page and network control work, Projector Pilot should reach it too.
Purchases & restore

Buying, and getting it back

Is it a one-time purchase?

Yes. Pro, the Home Automation add-on and the bundle are all one-time purchases. No subscription, no ads, no account. See pricing.

How do I restore on a new device?

Sign in to the same store account you bought on and use Restore Purchases in the app. One purchase unlocks every platform you own - Android, iOS, Windows and macOS - tied to that store account.

I already own it but the app shows an error

It should not, and newer builds fix exactly that. If the store reports Pro, Home Automation or the bundle as already owned, the app grants it and quietly refreshes your purchases, so buying or restoring something you already own always unlocks it.

Why is the price different from what I read online?

The app shows the store's price, in your own currency, before you confirm. Regional pricing differs, and the app never falls back to a hard-coded figure - if the store has not answered yet it says so rather than inventing a number.

Privacy

Your data stays yours

Projector Pilot is a local app. It talks to your projector on your network, and there is nothing to sign up for.

  • No account, no cloud. You do not create an account, and the projector's data is not sent to any server of ours.
  • Crash diagnostics via Crashlytics. When the app crashes it sends a diagnostic report so the bug can be fixed. That is standard crash reporting, not tracking.
  • Optional, opt-in analytics. Usage analytics are off by default. The app asks once on first launch, and you can turn them on or off any time in Settings › About. Nothing is collected or sent unless you turn it on.
  • Nothing is sold. Your data is not sold to anyone, full stop.
